Why 13 WAVs?

One source sound. Thirteen played notes.

The game usually stores one short recording per icon. The audio chip reads it at different speeds to produce notes. We exported the finished result at each of the composer’s 13 staff positions, so you can hear its exact pitch choices and volume envelope without rebuilding the SNES sound engine.

01

Sample

A tiny BRR-compressed recording provides the timbre: a bark, a bell, a voice or an instrument.

02

Pitch & loop

Read faster for a higher pitch; slower for a lower pitch. A loop can repeat the sample’s tail to sustain it.

03

Envelope

Attack, decay, sustain and release shape its volume over time. Every new note starts a new playback voice.

The boat is the exception: its lower six staff positions use a drum sample; its upper seven use a cymbal. That makes 16 composer samples for 15 icons. The complete bank has 23 entries including auxiliary sounds.

For Bongo Planet, a compact sampler would load the source samples once and apply pitch, loop and envelope settings per trigger. The 195 WAVs are convenient rendered examples, rather than 195 independent instruments. Staff labels are visual score positions; sounding pitches differ across instruments.
