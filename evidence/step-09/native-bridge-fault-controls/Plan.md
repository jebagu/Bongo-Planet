## Independent case oracles

Use declared packet literals and expected states, not copied bridge branches. Custom node/generator/session/trigger strings are separate from positive integer object IDs. Trigger IDs end with their declared immutable triggerSequence (`custom:take-A-1`, `custom:take-A-2`, etc.). Position sequence, control sequence and triggerSequence are tracked as three explicit independent domains; position sequences can be7000/7001 while control sequences are10/11 and triggerSequence1/2. Heartbeats have no sequence. New epochs may restart these domains without replaying old work. Do not send a valid current-epoch packet with a stale control sequence merely to test an unrelated epoch condition.

Each live setup uses one real onset deliberately queued about400ms ahead, a two-second numerical voice, and another future trigger about1.4seconds ahead. First wait for actual worker onset and bridge active voice, while confirming the second trigger is admitted in both queues. A skipped, late-dropped, missing or clock-failed setup fails the case; it never counts as a cancellation success. No deadline is retargeted or receipt fabricated. Healthy-owner heartbeats run every100ms and are stopped explicitly before transport cut; they follow only the currently declared fresh epoch.

1. **Transport loss and stale epoch admission.** Establish epochA with position, active voice and future trigger. Terminate only the owned producer WebSocket without sending stop. Require observed socket close and bridge stop/reason for this epoch; require native worker release row for the same epoch, its later applied acknowledgment and a newer authoritative empty worker state. Bridge and worker must both show empty session/objects/pending/voices. A new owned observer peer sends epochA start, position, trigger and cancel packets; each must receive an error/rejected record and must not create a session, UDP position or onset. Continue observation beyond the original future deadline plus100ms, retaining all native rows and bridge records; no epochA future onset may execute.

2. **Fresh-peer receiver synchronization and protection from old traffic.** The new peer performs a real version1sync request with retained t0/server/t1; all three are finite and causally ordered where applicable. Sync alone must not restore epochA. Start epochB and explicitly send its independently declared current positions (including a changed coordinate and chosen stable integer object ID). Require actual raw UDP address/float payload match and fresh worker epoch/objects. Establish epochB active/future work. Inject epochA start/control/position/trigger whileB is current; each must be rejected, with unchangedB session/object snapshot/pending IDs and noA UDP/onsets. Afterwards send a validB position update and trigger to prove the rejected traffic did not corrupt its independent sequence domains. This is receiver-side acceptance of a manually supplied snapshot, not automatic adapter reconnect behavior.

3. **Clear and scene-epoch replacement.** WhileB has an observed active voice and admitted future trigger, send explicit stop/reason `Clear` with the next valid control sequence. Require bridge stop and native release/applied acknowledgment plus both empty states. Observe beyondB’s original queued deadline and reject subsequent retiredB packets. StartC with its declared position and new active/future pair; replace it on the same producer with fresh startD and onlyD’s declared snapshot. RequireC’s worker release beforeD’s started/applied rows and currentD state with noC queue/voices/objects. Send retiredC controls/positions/triggers and require rejections withoutD corruption. Observe pastC’s original future deadline; noC future onset may execute. A deliberate freshD trigger must execute exactly once withD’s identity/XYZ, then explicit stopD leaves both states empty before graceful bridge close.

For all three, pending IDs/actual onset rows are retained; bridge counts alone cannot prove that the worker released. A pre-stop onset already executed but reported later is not declared stale execution: compare its actualHrNs/mutation witness to release/stop-applied order. Future cancellation assertions use a deadline strictly after the retained stop acknowledgment, with at least300ms separation, and observe past that original deadline. If setup/ordering cannot supply this separation, fail instead of excluding the event. Native clock unready/failure/uncertainty>10ms or a late-drop is explicit failure, with all witnesses retained; scheduling policy stays±20ms.
