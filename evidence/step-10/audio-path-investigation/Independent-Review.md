## What the clock evidence does and does not establish

At each product repeat, the observer's `currentFrame` repeats its previous value, then advances 256 rather than 128 frames on the next block. `currentTime * 48000` agrees with that same frame value within 1.862646e-9 samples. Therefore this is not merely floating-point rounding of a timestamp. Both paths' clock-discontinuity lists come from the same dual-input observer metadata; they are not independent clock witnesses.

The retained observer immediately copies each input to a fresh array, supplies an independent incrementing block index, batches 32 blocks with an independent sequence, and transfers the buffers. The capture checks sequence/lengths before appending. The product's original destination connection remains; a parallel connection supplies observer input 0. Named virtual input supplies input 1. Observer output is zero, avoiding doubled product sound.

The production processor uses `currentTime` as the start argument to engine advancement and synthesis. If it actually receives the repeated time seen downstream, a deterministic voice can be rendered at the same ages twice, then skip an interval on the following jump. That is a concrete hypothesis, not an observed production read: this capture does not instrument the value inside `BongoProcessor.process()`. Graph-level reuse or another shared-clock effect remains possible. Because `currentFrame` also repeats in the observer, simply substituting `currentFrame / sampleRate` for `currentTime` is not an evidenced fix.

The matching pre-device and input data make an input-only or driver-only copying explanation insufficient for these events. They do not rule out the virtual route as a stimulus that affects the graph's shared clock. They also do not establish audible harm, the duration of an output-device interruption, or fault attribution to Chrome versus application synthesis.

## Sample-cursor source review — before the repaired build

Reviewed the proposed production `src/audio/processor.ts`, fourteen controlled cases in `tests/processor-clock.test.ts`, and the fatal-state guards and four adapter cases in `tests/audio-adapter.test.ts`. This was a source review; this reviewer did not launch another browser or audio capture, or rerun the suite during the concurrent work.

The processor now floors both rendering and command time at the previous rendered block end and the engine's current time. Fresh callbacks therefore advance synthesis even when the supplied global clock repeats. The correction is bounded: nonfinite clock values or backward lag exceeding 20 ms plus 0.01 sample permanently silence the processor and report an explicit reload error. Forward interruption and deliberate-play warmup remain separate. Commands cannot advance the engine and then let rendering move behind that command.

The regressions execute the actual processor and synth with controlled worklet globals. One- and two-repeat cases compare PCM against an independently advancing sample-count clock, compare emitted musical times, check unique trigger/onset IDs, and separately calculate the first onset from the dot-product angle, travel fraction, tempo and warmup quantum. The new command-ahead case covers a command occurring halfway beyond the preceding block. Other cases cover edit/pause, paused load/Clear, the lag threshold, persistent frozen clocks, nonfinite cleanup, and forward interruption/resume. These assertions give useful independent timing expectations; the waveform comparison uses pitched-note voices, not a fresh live F06/F08 recording.

One adapter race was found and repaired during review. After a native or reported fatal processor failure, a delayed reset event could replace the reload message with resumable interruption/capacity guidance. Both reset guidance branches now require absence of `processorFailure`. The updated tests retain the fatal detail while still acknowledging and forwarding the delayed reset batch; pause, load, Play and Enable cannot pretend to recover the failed processor. Reload is the explicit recovery boundary.

There remains an unqualified cross-clock boundary. Engine/trigger times now follow a bounded corrected cursor, while `OutputAdapter` still converts them through the main thread's `AudioContext.currentTime`. If that main-thread clock lags the corrected cursor, the outgoing deadline can acquire that lag in addition to the measured performance-to-Node synchronization error. The existing traces prove repeated worklet globals; they do not prove that the main-thread clock repeats identically. Accordingly this is an unmeasured relationship requiring direct correlation, not an observed receiver error or justification for a speculative offset correction. Eight producer sync probes do not measure this audio-to-performance relationship. Fresh local PCM, receiver controls, continuity and human listening retain their separate gates.

## Original-mixture timing: measurement design and identifiability limits

This is a read-only measurement proposal, not a new capture or a gate pass. It follows `Rules.md`'s actual-output requirement and the original fixtures, not the earlier short-duration/silent-sentinel variants. The old harness also calibrated from product onsets; the replacement must use native sources independent of the product so a constant product delay cannot disappear into calibration.

Independent arithmetic over the saved fixture JSON and topology vectors, using dot-product angles, declared travel and nearest-quarter-beat quantization, gives the following recurring onset groups. No Engine or synth code was executed for this enumeration.

| Fixture | Offsets within cycle, seconds | Trigger counts at those offsets | Cycle, seconds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F02 | 0.125 | 3 | 2 |
| F03 | 0.125, 0.5, 0.625 | 1, 1, 1 | 2 |
| F06 | 0, 0.125, 0.25, 0.375 | 16, 152, 158, 26 | 0.5 |
| F08 | 0, 1/12, 1/6, 1/4 | 20, 284, 314, 54 | 1/3 |

There is a strict information limit. `processor.ts` applies all due events before rendering the block. `MonoSynth.start()` repeatedly steals the oldest voice at capacity64. Consequently F06's152/158-event batches erase at least88/94 of their new voices before any sample; F08's284/314-event batches erase at least220/250. Deleting or duplicating an early doomed `start()` inside one of these batches can leave the final64-voice state and all PCM unchanged. These intentionally inaudible events cannot have independently measured PCM onsets. Also, every original dense beat is the same kick; same-onset kicks and same-pitch notes with equal settings are identical functions regardless of node/generator ID. Swapping their identities leaves the recording unchanged. IDs seed only the hat/snare noise paths, which these original fixtures do not use.

A workable target is **individually identified audible onset groups**, plus independent logical provenance and voice-steal evidence. For example, original F02 for64seconds, F03 for24seconds, and F06/F08 for20seconds each provide roughly32/36/160/240 groups before explicit edge exclusions. This comfortably exceeds120 if identification succeeds. A chord or dense simultaneous batch counts once as a measured time, regardless of its trigger multiplicity; F02's three pitches can additionally be checked as distinct spectral components. Include actual100/250ms main-thread stalls spanning predetermined interior groups of each dense fixture, retaining measured stall intervals. Analyze every eligible group in each frozen window rather than selecting the best120. Report fixture-level distributions as well as the combined count.

The concrete method is:

1. Bind unchanged fixture JSON, topology, main and processor bytes before/after capture. Retain the unmodified product tap and exact named virtual-input PCM with independent sample indices and every-quantum metadata. Native isolated `AudioBufferSource` clicks bypass the product and establish the sample-time/route intercept before playback. Repeat native probes after playback to test the same fixed intercept; retain drift/discontinuity and calibration uncertainty rather than fitting a slope or a new product-dependent intercept. A sample-count axis must not silently inherit known repeated frame headers. Any loss, unexplained time-axis inconsistency or route change invalidates absolute timing.
2. Compute all expected times and voice ownership independently from original geometry/settings, generator order, onset/sequence ordering and the documented64-voice rule. The first transport emission may supply the declared musical epoch, but no product trigger/onset list supplies expected delays or measured audio arrivals. Explicitly distinguish this transport-relative check from gesture-to-sound latency. Derive C-major pitches independently from MIDI/semitone constants. All relevant original voices are sine notes or kicks, permitting a compact independent analytic waveform model without importing `MonoSynth`.
3. Model the entire simultaneous mixture in output-sample space: note attack2ms, note envelope/level, kick chirp `48*a + 2.31*(1-exp(-a/0.022))`, kick decay, deterministic stealing, master gain, and final `0.98*tanh(sum*gain)`. Keep frequencies, levels, envelope durations and multiplicities fixed by the oracle. Model removal of a stolen old voice at block admission separately from the new voice's within-block onset. The removal discontinuity alone is not the new note's audible onset. Do not invert `tanh` at rounded saturation plateaus; forward-model Float32 output and retain near-saturation uncertainty/information loss.
4. Estimate an onset from a predeclared local window containing both the pre-onset mixture and the new component's entrance, conditioning on the preceding mixture. Search actual entrance time and retain the full plausible-fit interval, alternative minima and residuals. A shifted full-waveform/phase correlation is insufficient: `MonoSynth` computes age from the committed onset even when a voice is admitted late. A10ms-late insertion can omit its first10ms yet preserve the nominal later phase. The analyzer must therefore distinguish translated phase/envelope from late gate opening with the original phase, and test missing and duplicate component/group alternatives. Long periodic tails alone cannot identify an attack. F03 can use its distinct known C/E/G components despite overlap; the dense mixtures require the complete simultaneous model and counterfactual comparisons.
5. Freeze calibration before evaluating product data. Per-group fitted shifts are the measured errors and must never be recentered by their median or absorbed into an arbitrary phase/gain/time warp. Retain raw sample bounds, fitted entrance bounds, residual quality, alternative/no-onset likelihood and route uncertainty for every group. A conservative timing bound includes the widest plausible entrance error plus independent calibration/sample-axis uncertainty. Ambiguous or unidentifiable groups remain explicit failures/unqualified observations, not zero-error samples or exclusions chosen after seeing the data. Detect unmatched audible structure and assess all expected groups; periodicity makes nearest-peak matching alone unsafe.
6. Validate the analyzer before relying on it: independent synthetic known-time mixtures must distinguish 5/10/20ms translations, late insertions with unchanged phase, deletions, duplicates, whole-batch timing errors, saturation and periodic aliases. Preserve deliberate unidentifiable cases as such. Then repeat an actual product-only100ms DelayNode negative control with unchanged native calibration, which must fail absolute timing. A model generated with the production synth itself cannot be the only positive truth set. Direct-tap/input byte agreement at the independently calibrated route lag can establish faithful transmission of the observed mixture, but does not independently establish its musical timing.

This method is feasible to attempt and can establish more than120 measured **audible group times** if its retained fit/negative-control evidence demonstrates identifiability. It cannot turn the unchanged dense mono signal into independent per-ID evidence, prove a doomed voice was rendered, or count a phase-locked chord as three independent timing observations. A full pass of missing/duplicate-event atoms therefore still needs explicitly scoped logical event/ownership evidence alongside PCM and an agreed interpretation of audible versus deliberately stolen events. The present acceptance ledger already leaves the dense individual-onset boundary open. No requirements, fixtures or ledger statuses were changed by this review.
