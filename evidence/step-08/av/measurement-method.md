# Bongo Planet — independently calibrated F02 audiovisual measurement

The corrected capture on commit `9621617296ffeacf1e27c84f65e8da949a641c69` matched all **44 independently expected onset groups** in the original F02 scene. The conservative audiovisual error statistic was **27.202 ms median, 28.807 ms p95, 28.955 ms maximum**. This passes the measured automated subset of median ≤50 ms and p95 ≤100 ms, with at least 30 triggers. Human observation, physical monitor presentation, other fixtures, and the full dense audio timing gate remain open.

The installed Chrome browser captured the clean production page at the permanent URL `http://127.0.0.1:5417/BongoPlanet/`. Both before and after capture, the harness fetched the actual HTML, its referenced main JavaScript, and the processor URL referenced by that main JavaScript. The served main SHA-256 was `e0985953a9a9dc53c99a60420ea3ce3e4cf762c5c53056e9a7dafc41397fe85f`; the served processor was `155ba49f4f3e41921a86ae4adaa5595bcfe2858f5ebbadc7b28fa3281b50607d`. The complete capture-time script is retained in `capture-harness.ts`; the current development harness can have later negative-control additions.

The recorder selected the exact named **SPAT 8ch loopback (Virtual)** input and output device IDs. Output routing used this test AudioContext's `setSinkId`; system default input and output were unchanged. No ambient microphone, default-input fallback, fake audio device, or synth replay was used. The raw mono Float32 PCM and WAV contain the actual first channel of the selected virtual input at 48 kHz. The returned input track had two channels. Browser command arguments confirm that Chrome's default `--mute-audio` flag was omitted.

Before product playback, twelve native `AudioBufferSourceNode` clicks were scheduled at known context sample times, 300 ms apart. Each used a 1 ms rectangular buffer connected directly to the same context destination. Their independently detected capture offsets were 13.333333 ms, with negligible variation at retained numerical precision. This fixed route offset was measured from native clicks and then applied to product measurements. Product onsets were not used to estimate the calibration constant. All 44 product output residuals against fixture-derived expected times were +0.083333 ms after this calibration.

Expected product onset times were derived from the untouched F02 generator/note vertices, topology unit vectors, angular distance, travel, nearest quantization grid, tempo, and generator period. The first emission provided the session's time origin. The fixed 88-second product window yields 44 groups independently of recorded onset count. The detector used an absolute PCM threshold of 0.001 after at least 50 ms of silence; exact count matching rejected missing and extra groups. The three simultaneous original chord notes share each audible attack group. The ordinary camera drag exposed the original visible target `chord-2`, vertex 10, without editing the composition.

Actual compositor frames were recorded with CDP screencast JPEGs, then decoded for their real pixels. A fixed 25×25 target ROI was scored for white flash pixels: R>180, G>170, B>160, at least three pixels. Each measured onset requires a preceding dark frame followed by the first bright frame. The retained interval brackets the visual change; both endpoints are compared with the independently calibrated audible onset. The reported error is the larger absolute endpoint error plus the maximum observed output-clock mapping deviation, 0.104000 ms. It is not a renderer callback timestamp or a comparison of two product scheduling logs.

`AudioContext.getOutputTimestamp()` maps context sample positions into the page's performance clock. `performance.timeOrigin` converts that mapping to the epoch clock used by CDP frame metadata. The W3C defines this as an estimated output-device presentation mapping; observed mapping variation is retained rather than fitted away. CDP metadata's timestamp describes the frame swap, rather than the later screenshot encoding or delivery time. These are headless compositor pixels, not a photodiode measurement of a physical display.

The full retained capture contains 2,173 atomic PCM chunks, **4,450,304 samples**, and 5,566 actual JPEG frames. Raw sample count and chunk metadata sample count match exactly; maximum inter-chunk timestamp deviation is below half a sample. Recorder timestamps derive from the integer worklet `currentFrame` sample counter. The AudioContext was closed before PCM analysis, all outstanding chunk writes drained, and the browser was closed at **2026-10-11T18:18:08.637Z**. The WAV uses identical sample bytes to the raw PCM. `report.json`, `frames.json`, `capture-chunks.json`, `clock.json`, `pixel-scores.json`, and `measurements.json` preserve the complete measurements and provenance.

Earlier attempts remain separate. `attempt-02-assumed-calibration` used product onsets for calibration and lacked served-byte attestation; it qualifies only relative consistency under its assumed latency. `attempt-03-current-time-irregularity` retained exact PCM/metadata counts but failed strict sample-clock continuity because one chunk's floating context-time timestamp produced a −128/+128-sample pair. Neither attempt qualifies absolute timing. The later actual [100 ms product-only delay negative control](negative-control-delay100/measurement-method.md) correctly rejected all six measured product groups at +100.083333 ms while its independent native calibration remained 13.333333 ms. This demonstrates that this calibration method does not subtract away the injected constant product delay. Integer-frame timestamping does not itself guarantee continuity; the final soak's retained recording independently failed its strict continuity check.
